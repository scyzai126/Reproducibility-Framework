Same-term flow cytometry entries: keep apart or merge?

2026-11-20 · Measured read-only on assay_result_v3 (31 papers, benchmark v5). No pipeline run, no code change.

When one paper runs several different measurements on a flow cytometer, should the assay list hold one entry per measurement (current behaviour) or one flow cytometry assay entry per paper?

Keeping them apart scores 88.6% precision against the curated benchmark. Merging scores about 93.3% but loses three rows the curators wrote.

Why it comes up

The ontology cannot tell these measurements apart, so the agent does it with its own fields.

  • An OBI term names a technique, not what was measured. Immunophenotyping, intracellular cytokine staining (ICS), phosphoflow and a degranulation assay all map to flow cytometry assay.
  • A larger OBI vocabulary does not separate them. With the full 1,794-term list, 36 of 38 flow entries still map to flow cytometry assay. The finer terms that exist are per-cytokine, epitope-specific T-cell ICS terms; there is none for immunophenotyping, phosphoflow, chemokine-receptor staining or NK degranulation.
  • The agent therefore separates entries by what was measured (readouts) and on what specimen (sample_type), not by the term.

For A

The extra entries are real experiments (8 of 9 confirmed), and merging hides them and loses three rows the curators did write. Its lower precision reflects the benchmark’s grain, not errors: counting the confirmed same-term entries as correct gives about 97%.

For B

It matches how the curators wrote most papers, scores higher against the benchmark, and gives a shorter list of techniques per paper.

A middle form is possible: one entry per technique that lists its measurements inside it. It needs a schema change and a scorer change, so it is a follow-up rather than a third answer today.

The same pattern exists for other techniques, so the answer should say whether it applies to all of them or to flow cytometry alone.
