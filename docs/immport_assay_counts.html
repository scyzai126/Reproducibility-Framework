Why 322 studies give 999 Flow Cytometry experiments

A study registers one experiment for each staining panel or cell type it measured, so a single study can count several times. Two examples from the database:

Of the 322 studies, 183 registered Flow Cytometry once, 72 twice, 40 three to five times, and 27 six times or more. Four studies alone account for 266 of the 999 experiments.

Would the full OBI list separate SDY1252’s five experiments?

In ImmPort all five map to the same term, OBI:0000916 flow cytometry assay, because the OBI link is stored once per technique name, not per experiment. Mapping against the full OBI assay list (1,794 terms) instead would not change that for four of the five.

EXP31081DC/Mono/NKPartial: monocyte assay (OBI:0003025) and natural killer cell count assay (OBI:0003722). Neither is a flow cytometry term, and there is none for dendritic cells.

What OBI has under “flow cytometry assay”. It has 50 more specific child terms, but none of them fit:

  • 49 are epitope-specific assays, such as “intracellular cytokine staining assay measuring epitope specific interleukin-6 production by T cells”. They describe antigen-stimulation experiments, not immunophenotyping panels.
  • 1 is “T cell activation induced marker assay” (OBI:0003230), which detects antigen-specific T cells after stimulation. It is not a general T cell panel.

The five experiments differ in which cell population was measured, not in technique. OBI assay terms describe the technique, so a longer list of them cannot separate these panels. This was checked by reading the vocabulary, not by running the mapper on the five experiments.
