Assay-to-OBI Mapping Workflow

2026-10-21 · round 4 of the assay pipeline, embedding strategy (the default)

Overview

Round 4 of the assay pipeline assigns every assay a paper reports to one term in OBI, the Ontology for Biomedical Investigations, so that assays from different papers can be compared and folded on a shared vocabulary. The current strategy, embedding, resolves each assay name in at most three network calls: an exact lookup against the OBI vocabulary, a vector-similarity retrieval of the twenty closest terms, and one LLM call that picks among them. On the 31-paper curated benchmark it agrees with the curator's term on 134 of 149 matched assays (89.9%).

The vocabulary is the assay branch of OBI pruned to 701 generic terms: 545 analyte-specific constructs ("ELISPOT assay measuring epitope specific IFN-gamma production by T cells") and 548 out-of-scope domain terms are removed, because they outrank the plain technique terms in similarity search and were the source of most wrong picks before the prune. Each term carries its label, synonyms, definition, descendant count and direct is_a parents.

Inputs per assay come from the extraction rounds: the paper's own name for the assay, its other wordings and aliases, the extractor's judgement of the standard technique, the sample type and the readouts. Only the paper's name is used as an exact key; everything else reaches the model as evidence.

Workflow

Step by step

Each assay goes through the steps below in order; the first that answers ends the chain.

  1. Exact lookup (free). The paper's own name, lowercased, is looked up against every OBI label and synonym; a trailing " assay" is ignored on both sides. A hit on a term with no children in the vocabulary is returned at once (resource exact_original). A hit on a term that has children ("RNA-seq", "flow cytometry") is not returned: papers name assays by the bare technique word, which is usually the parent term, so the hit and its direct children are pinned into the candidate list for step 4 to decide.
  2. Vector retrieval. Two queries are embedded with text-embedding-3-small and scored by cosine similarity against the 701 pre-embedded terms (each embedded as label, synonyms and definition): the paper's wording, name | alias 1 | alias 2 ..., and the extractor's standard technique name, only when it differs from the paper's name. Each query returns its top 20. The two pools are merged into one list of 20, with 14 slots reserved for the paper-wording pool so the technique query's broad, high-scoring terms cannot push out the specific ones. There is no similarity threshold; declining is the model's job.
  3. Neighbour expansion. For the three best-retrieved terms, their direct OBI parents and children are appended (children only when a term has eight or fewer). Retrieval ranks the term a wording resembles, which is often a parent or sibling of the right one; adding the neighbours lets the model make the parent-versus-child choice itself instead of leaving it to the ranking.
  4. One LLM pick. The model (the pipeline's configured chat model, temperature 0, fixed seed) sees the assay record and every candidate as one line: id, label, up to four synonyms, definition (capped at 300 characters), descendant count, and a mark saying why it is there (retrieved with its similarity, exact name match, child or parent of candidate N). The prompt's rules, in order of weight: judge by definition rather than label; choose the most specific term the record's evidence warrants; the assay name outranks readouts and sample type; aliases and technique name are evidence, not overrides; prefer a correct broad category to no answer. Domain rules cover array assays, functional assays read on a cytometer, and multiplex bead immunoassays. The reply is N EXACT, N BROAD or none.
  5. Result. The chosen term is written with its id, label, the cosine score as obi_confidence, the model's EXACT or BROAD, and a resource that says how it was reached: exact_original_llm when an exact hit was pinned, embedding_llm_neighbour when only the neighbour expansion could reach the term, embedding_llm_normalized when only the technique query retrieved it, embedding_llm otherwise. A none reply falls back to the pinned exact hit when there is one and is unresolved otherwise; an unreadable reply is recorded as an error so the entry can be re-run.

Every candidate list and reply is kept in the run's assays_raw.json, so a wrong term is diagnosed from disk rather than by paying for the call again.

Of the 15 disagreements, 6 are sibling terms (RT-PCR against real-time RT-PCR, bulk against polyA-selected RNA-seq), 4 are a parent chosen where the curator chose a child or the reverse, and 5 have no curator term to compare with.

Cost per assay: an exact hit on a leaf term is free. Every other assay costs one embedding call for the paper's wording, a second when a technique name differs, and one chat call to pick: 2 to 3 calls. An 8-assay paper with one free hit spends about 21 calls on this round. Retrieval alone puts the curator's term among the 20 candidates for 91% of benchmark rows; the remaining accuracy is the pick.

Alternatives evaluated

Two tree-based strategies were implemented in October 2026 and compared with the stored embedding mapping on the three papers where it disagreed most with the curator (SDY1264, SDY1373, SDY1260; 18 curated assays). Neither beat it, so embedding remains the default; both stay selectable through OBI_MAPPING_STRATEGY for further runs.

strategyhow it workssame term as curatorcalls spent
embedding (default)retrieve 20, one pick11 of 1841 (stored run)
descendwalk the OBI tree from the top, one LLM call per level choosing among a term's direct children10 of 1837
hybridthe embedding pick, then one descent from it when the picked term has children11 of 1811

The tree walk fails where the ontology's is_a placement differs from the category a reader would enter: serum neutralization of viral infectivity sits under analyte assay, not pathogen neutralization; transcription profiling by array under transcription profiling, not microarray assay. A walk that takes the obvious branch cannot reach the term; retrieval finds it by its label regardless of branch. The hybrid avoids that but over-specified one assay onto a term's only child while fixing one parent-for-child case, for no net change. Across the whole corpus only 3 rows are a parent-for-child disagreement, so no descent variant can move the benchmark by more than 2 rows.
