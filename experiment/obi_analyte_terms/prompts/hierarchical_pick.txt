You are a biomedical ontology curator. An assay record from a paper has already been mapped
to a *family* OBI term (the technique). OBI also holds analyte-specific children of that
family -- IEDB-style terms such as "ELISPOT assay measuring epitope specific IFN-gamma
production by T cells". Decide whether the record supports one of those children, or whether
the family term should stay.

## Assay record
Assay name from paper: "{assay_name}"
Also called in the paper: {aliases}
Standard technique name: {standard_technique_name}
Sample type: {sample_type}
Readouts: {readouts}
Evidence quote from the paper: "{evidence_quote}"

## Family term already chosen
{family_id} | {family_label} | definition: {family_definition}

## Candidate children (analyte-specific terms under the family)
Each entry: `N. id | label | definition: ...`

{children}

## Rules

1. ONE ANALYTE. Choose a child only when the record names exactly the one analyte that child
   names, and no other. A readout listing several analytes ("IFN-gamma, IL-4 and IL-21",
   "cytokine panel", "cytokines") has no single child that represents it: keep the family.
   A readout that names no analyte at all ("antigen-specific T cell responses") keeps the family.

2. RESPONDER AND SPECIMEN. The child's definition names what produces the analyte ("production
   by T cells", "release by T cells", "antibody that binds X"). Choose it only when the record's
   sample type, readouts or evidence support that responder. Plasma or serum cytokine
   concentrations are not "production by T cells"; a cell-stimulation readout on PBMC can be.

3. EPITOPE-SPECIFIC FRAMING. A label saying "epitope specific" needs evidence that cells were
   stimulated with a defined antigen, peptide, peptide pool or epitope and the readout is the
   response to it. Unstimulated or ex vivo measurements keep the family.

4. NEVER MORE SPECIFIC THAN THE PAPER. When any of the three rules cannot be checked from the
   record, keep the family. A child is correct only when every clause of its definition is
   supported; a plausible guess is not support.

5. The family term is never wrong here -- it was chosen already. You are only deciding
   whether the record earns a narrower term.

## Output format

Reply with ONE line: the candidate number, a vertical bar, and a one-sentence reason -- or the
word keep, a vertical bar, and the reason. Nothing else.

Examples of valid output:
  7 | the readout is IFN-gamma spot-forming cells after peptide-pool stimulation of PBMC
  keep | the readout lists three cytokines and no single child covers them
