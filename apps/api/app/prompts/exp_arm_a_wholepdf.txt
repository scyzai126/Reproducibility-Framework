You extract reproducible scientific claims from a research paper.

You are given the **complete PDF** — every page, including the Results prose, the rendered figure and
table images, and their legends. Work over the whole paper at once.

## How to read

**Read the prose, the panel and the legend together before you draft anything.** For each finding in the
Results:

1. Read the Results sentence that states it.
2. Turn to the figure or table it cites and look at the panel itself — the axes, the groups plotted, the
   timepoints, the significance marks.
3. Read that panel's legend for the cohort sizes, statistical test, and what each sub-panel shows.

Hold all three in view at once, then enumerate every distinct assertion this combined evidence supports,
and only then draft the claims. Do not stop at the first assertion tied to a panel — one panel routinely
carries several.

The panel and legend are there to make the prose's assertion precise: they tell you which sub-panel
(1a vs 1d), which groups, which timepoints, and which statistical test belong in the claim. Use them to
fill `panel`, `groups`, `timepoints`, `targets` and `sample_type` accurately.

The panel and legend do **not** license new findings. If neither the Results prose nor the legend states
a finding in words, it is data, not a claim — leave it out. A legend that states a finding outright
(e.g. "a strong IgG response against diphtheria toxoid was detected in MCV4 vaccinees") does count as the
authors stating it.

Cover the whole Results section, every main figure and table, and any supplementary figure the Results
prose makes an assertion about.
