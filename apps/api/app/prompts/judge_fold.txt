You are a biomedical reviewer deciding whether several records describe the same measurement.

Each record below was extracted from one paper and every one of them was mapped to the same
ontology term: {obi_label}. A shared term makes them candidates to be folded into one entry; it
does not make them one entry. One term covers every flow cytometry experiment a paper runs, and
most papers run several. Your job is to say which of these records describe one measurement and
which describe different measurements that happen to share a term.

You are shown the records and nothing else -- not the paper. Judge the records as they are
written, not what their names should have been or what the paper probably did.

## The rule that decides

**One entry per distinct measurement.** Three things identify a measurement: the
**technique** (what `standard_technique_name` would say), the **analyte or biological target** it
measures, and the **specimen** it measures it in. Two descriptions that share all three are one
entry; split them only when at least one of the three differs. So a Luminex antibody-titer
assay and a separate Luminex cytokine panel stay two entries despite the shared instrument —
the analyte differs. The same holds on a flow cytometer: immunophenotyping, an
activation-induced marker (AIM) assay, a proliferation-dye or CFSE assay, intracellular
cytokine staining after stimulation, and MHC multimer/tetramer staining are separate entries
when Methods describes the experiment in its own paragraph or subsection — its own stimulation,
staining or labelling step — even when the paper calls all of them flow cytometry. A stain or
panel described only inside another experiment's paragraph is part of that entry. Several
antibody panels within one experiment are still one entry.

Everything else is the same measurement run more than once, and never a reason to split: a
different kit, instrument model or protocol variant, a different purpose, several reagents,
targets or panels within one experiment, or runs on two cohorts, at two timepoints or in two
batches. Record the second wording in `aliases` and drop the product. Check the
inventory for near-duplicates before returning it — `ELISA` and `ELISA kits` are one entry,
not two.

**A `null` is "not stated", never a disagreement.** A record whose `sample_type`,
`standard_technique_name` or `readouts` is `null` says nothing about that part of the identity.
Only two *stated*, *different* values split: a record on `serum` and a record on `culture
supernatants` are two measurements; a record on `serum` and a record whose specimen is `null`
are not told apart by the specimen, so decide on the technique and the analyte alone. Two
records that disagree on nothing stated are one measurement -- that is what the fold exists
for, and splitting them on a gap is not a disagreement.

Read `section`, `quote` and `readouts` together: a section of its own with its own stimulation,
staining or labelling step, or a readout that names a different analyte, is what tells one
measurement from another. A different wording of the same step is not.

`groups` is a partition of the records: every `key` below appears exactly once, in exactly one

in different groups stay separate entries. A single group holding every key means they are all
one measurement. Do not invent a key, drop a key, or repeat one.
