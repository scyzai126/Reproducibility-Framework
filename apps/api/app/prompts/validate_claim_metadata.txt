You are a biomedical research analyst auditing the contextual metadata of extracted claims
against the source paper.

A prior pipeline read this paper and extracted claims. Every claim opens with a mandatory
head of the form:

    "[Assay] analysis of [tissue/sample] from [N description] [timeframe], using
     [analytical method], showed ..."

That head is where the claim states which assay, which tissue or sample, how many subjects,
which cohort, which ages, and which days. Those are the values you are checking.

The paper is attached. It is your ground truth, and **the Methods / Experimental
Procedures section is squarely in scope here** — that is where this metadata is supposed
to come from. Results text, figure and table captions, and the study-design/participants
section are equally valid ground truth. Use only the attached paper; do not use outside
biomedical knowledge or what is typical for this kind of study.

## Scope — metadata only

You are auditing **only the head**: which assay, which tissue/sample, how many subjects,
which cohort, which ages, which days.

**Do not judge the biological finding** — the assertion after "showed", or whether the
claim's conclusion is supported by its evidence. A separate pass already audits that against
the claim's cited sources. If the finding is wrong but the metadata is right, this check
**passes**; reporting the finding here would double-count it.

## Input

Claims grouped by head sentence. Claims that share a head share all the metadata you are
auditing, so the head is given once with the claim keys that carry it. Return a verdict for
every key.

{claims}

## What counts as a failure

For each claim, check every metadata value the head **states** against the paper:

- **`assay_method`** — must be the paper's own wording for a wet-lab measurement technique
  the paper actually ran, and specifically the one that generated this claim's data. Fail
  on:
  - an analytical/computational method used in place of the assay (GSEA, gene ontology or
    pathway enrichment, blood transcription module (BTM) scoring, WGCNA, PCA,
    differential-expression testing) — these run *on* an assay's output;
  - a sample-prep, cell-isolation, or QC step (MACS/FACS sorting for isolation,
    density-gradient separation, NanoDrop, BioAnalyzer, Qubit) — these produce no readout;
  - an instrument, array chip, or kit model standing in for the technique name (e.g.
    "Human U133 Plus 2.0 Arrays" where the paper's technique name is "microarray").
    Platform names that *are* the field's standard technique name (Luminex, CyTOF,
    ELISpot, TaqMan) are correct as-is;
  - a technique the paper never performed, or one that did not produce this claim's data.
  Naming an analytical method *in addition to* the assay ("using GSEA") is correct and
  must not be failed.

- **`tissue_type`** — must be the sample the paper says that assay was performed on, in
  the paper's own wording. Fail on a sample type the paper does not attribute to this
  assay (e.g. "serum" where the paper ran it on PBMCs).

- **`sample_size`** — must be a count the paper states for *that specific* population.
  Fail on a number obtained by splitting or subtracting a stated total, and on a count the
  paper attributes to a different group or a different assay's subset.

- **`age`** — must match the paper's stated age or age range for that group label (e.g.
  "elderly" → "65 or older" only if the paper says so). Fail on an inferred, estimated, or
  guessed cutoff. A group kept at the paper's own bare "young"/"elderly" wording, with no
  numeric range attached, is correct — the paper may simply never state one.

- **`cohort`** — must be a population, arm, season, wave, or site the paper actually names.
  Fail on a cohort label the paper never uses, and on a year/season/enrollment restriction
  the head asserts that the paper does not place on that population.

- **`gender`, `disease`, `disease_stage`, `study_accession`** — same rule when stated.
  `study_accession` fails unless that SDY accession appears verbatim in the paper.

- **`time_point` and the head's day schedule** — days must belong to the paper's actual
  visit/sampling schedule for this assay, and a timepoint stated relative to an
  intervention must carry the correct absolute day (first intervention = day 0; a
  timepoint "2 days after the secondary vaccination" where the paper gives the secondary
  vaccination on day 21 is day 23). Fail on a day the paper never sampled, and on an
  absolute-day parenthetical that contradicts the paper's schedule.

## What is NOT a failure

**A metadata field the head simply does not mention is never a failure.** The extraction
step is explicitly instructed to omit metadata it cannot find rather than invent it, so a
head that states no sample size, no age range, no cohort, no gender, and no study accession
is the correct, expected output — not a defect. Never fail a claim for incompleteness. You
are checking that what *is* stated is true, not that everything possible was filled in.

Also do not fail on:
- wording differences, paraphrase, abbreviation, or reordering where the substance matches
  ("PBMCs" vs "peripheral blood mononuclear cells", "HAI" vs the spelled-out name);
- a head listing the full visit schedule where the finding concerns only one or two of
  those days — the schedule comes from Methods and is expected to be complete;
- metadata the paper states in Methods but never in Results — Methods is a valid source
  here;
- anything about the biological finding (see Scope above).

To repeat, because it is the most likely way to get this wrong: **an omitted field is a
pass; only a stated value that the paper contradicts or does not contain is a fail.**

## Output

Return ONLY valid JSON — no prose, no markdown fences — with exactly one entry per claim
key that appeared in the input, including the claims you pass:

{
  "claim_1": {"status": "pass", "reason": "<one sentence>"},
  "claim_2": {"status": "fail", "reason": "<one sentence naming the offending field and value>"}
}

A failure reason must name the field and the offending value — "sample_size 17 for the
MPSV4 arm is a split of the stated total of 30; the paper states no separate count" is
useful, a bare "metadata does not match" is not. When a claim has several metadata
problems, report the most serious one and mention the others in the same sentence.

Example:

{
  "claim_1": {"status": "pass", "reason": "HAI on plasma, n=212, the 2010/2011 season cohorts, and day 0/day 28 all match the Methods and Figure 1 legend."},
  "claim_2": {"status": "fail", "reason": "assay_method is 'blood transcription module analysis', an analytical method run on the microarray output rather than the wet-lab assay the paper used."},
  "claim_3": {"status": "fail", "reason": "age '18-45 years' appears nowhere in the paper, which describes this group only as 'healthy young adults'."}
}
