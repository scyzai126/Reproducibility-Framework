## Claims

### claim_1

RNA-seq analysis of platelet-rich plasma from 65 participants across young adults (N=28), community-dwelling older adults (N=20), and skilled nursing facility-resident older adults (N=17) at pre-vaccination baseline (day 0), using differential expression and pathway enrichment analysis, showed increased platelet activation, degranulation, hemostasis, Rho GTPase effector, and mitochondrial RNA signatures and decreased translation-related RNAs in community-dwelling older adults compared to young adults, with further increases in platelet activation pathways and further decreases in translation-related RNAs in skilled nursing facility-resident older adults compared to community-dwelling older adults.

**Sources:**
- (Results 2.1) "Platelet activation pathways were significantly enriched in Older (Comm) compared with young adults, as were Rho GTPase effector pathways mediating the activation response (Aslan & McCarty, 2013) and mitochondrial genes (Table 2, Extended Data Figure S2b). Notably, activation pathways in platelets from Older (SNF) adults were also significantly increased compared to Older (Comm) adults."
- (Results 2.1) "In contrast to the age-associated increase in platelet activation RNAs, RNAs in pathways related to translation decreased with age and SNF resident status. These pathways were enriched in Young compared to Older (Comm) and Older (Comm) compared to Older (SNF) adults (Table 2, Tables S1–S4)."
- Table: Table 2

### claim_2

RNA-seq analysis of platelet-rich plasma from 65 participants across young adults (N=28), community-dwelling older adults (N=20), and skilled nursing facility-resident older adults (N=17) at days 0, 2, 7, and 28 after influenza vaccination (day 0), using non-negative CP tensor decomposition, showed distinct post-vaccination platelet activation RNA trajectories, with platelet activation genes including PF4 and PPBP increasing and remaining stable in young adults, dropping then returning toward baseline in community-dwelling older adults, and showing a sustained decrease in skilled nursing facility-resident older adults compared to baseline (day 0).

**Sources:**
- (Results 2.2) "Component 1 was associated with young adults, and represents a temporal expression pattern where RNA expression increased post-vaccination and remained stable. In Component 2, which was associated with Older (SNF) adults, pre-vaccination RNA levels experienced a sustained drop post-vaccination and in Component 5, associated with Older (Comm) adults, pre-vaccination RNA levels experienced a drop followed by a rise back to pre-vaccination levels."
- (Results 2.2) "Overall, these results showed that each group displays a distinct temporal pattern of RNAs associated with platelet activation."
- Figure: Figure 4

### claim_3

RNA-seq analysis of platelet-rich plasma from 65 participants across young adults (N=28), community-dwelling older adults (N=20), and skilled nursing facility-resident older adults (N=17) at days 0, 2, 7, and 28 after influenza vaccination (day 0), using non-negative CP tensor decomposition, showed that translation-related RNAs followed trajectories inverse to platelet activation and mitochondrial RNA programs, with higher pre-vaccination levels that decreased in young adults and lower pre-vaccination levels that increased in skilled nursing facility-resident older adults compared to baseline (day 0), while community-dwelling older adults showed intermediate dynamics.

**Sources:**
- (Results 2.2) "Top-scoring RNAs In Components 3 and 4 were also highly overlapping, with pathways involving RNA translation significantly over-represented. We observed that expression of these RNAs tended to have higher pre-vaccination levels which then decreased in young adults, while pre-vaccination levels were lower and increased in Older (SNF) adults, with intermediate dynamics in Older (Comm) adults (Extended Data Figure S3)."
- (Results 2.2) "Therefore, the negative correlation between platelet activation and translation gene RNA levels observed at pre-vaccination was maintained following vaccination."

### claim_4

flow cytometry analysis of platelet-rich plasma from young adults (n=14), community-dwelling older adults (n=17), and skilled nursing facility-resident older adults (n=17) at pre-vaccination baseline (day 0) and days 2 and 7 after influenza vaccination (day 0) showed significantly increased surface expression of the platelet activation markers CD62p, CD40L, and CD63 in community-dwelling older adults compared to young adults at all measured time points, whereas skilled nursing facility-resident older adults had significantly lower expression than community-dwelling older adults and levels largely comparable to young adults.

**Sources:**
- (Results 2.3) "The levels of surface expression of all three proteins were significantly increased in platelets from Older (Comm), compared to young adults at day 0, 2, and 7, suggesting an age-associated increase in platelet activation."
- (Results 2.3) "However, surface expression in Older (SNF) adults was significantly decreased compared to Older (Comm), with Older (SNF) activation marker surface expression comparable to that of platelets from young adults (with the exception of Day 0 CD62p expression in Older (SNF) adults, which was significantly higher compared to platelets from young adults) (Figure 5c,f)."
- Figure: Figure 5F

### claim_5

flow cytometry analysis of platelet-rich plasma from young adults (n=14), community-dwelling older adults (n=17), and skilled nursing facility-resident older adults (n=17) at days 2 and 7 after influenza vaccination (day 0), compared to baseline (day 0), showed that CD62p surface protein changes paralleled SELP RNA changes, with the highest fold-change in young adults, followed by community-dwelling older adults and then skilled nursing facility-resident older adults.

**Sources:**
- (Results 2.3) "We found that expression of SELP RNA at day 2 and day 7 post-vaccine, normalized to day 0, followed the pattern of platelet activation RNA levels identified by tensor decomposition analysis (Figure 4), with highest fold-change in young adults, followed by Older (Comm) and Older (SNF) adults (Figure 5b)."
- (Results 2.3) "Notably, the surface expression of CD62p/SELP protein at these post-vaccine time points relative to day 0 closely resembled the RNA expression pattern (Figure 5d)."
- Figure: Figure 5D

### claim_6

RNA-seq analysis of platelet-rich plasma from young adults classified as high responders (n=7) or low responders (n=9) at days 0, 2, 7, and 28 after influenza vaccination (day 0), using non-negative CP tensor decomposition and differential expression analysis, showed that platelet activation-associated RNAs including PF4 and PPBP decreased from days 7 to 28 in high responders but remained stable or were more highly expressed at day 28 in low responders compared to high responders.

**Sources:**
- (Results 2.4) "Among young high responders (n = 7), Component 2 RNAs decreased in expression on days 7-28, whereas in low responders (n = 9) the expression levels remained stable (Figure 6a)."
- (Results 2.4) "These genes include PF4 and PPBP and are enriched in pathways associated with platelet response (Figure 6b,c)."
- (Results 2.4) "The set of RNAs highly expressed in low responders at day 28 encode for proteins involved in pathways that include Rho GTPase effectors and platelet activation (Extended Data Figure S1,S4)."
- Figure: Figure 6A
- Figure: Figure 6C

### claim_7

hemagglutination inhibition assay analysis of serum from vaccinated participants showed that pre-vaccination platelet RNA expression differed between high and low vaccine responders, with 12 RNAs upregulated and 7 down-regulated in high responders compared to low responders.

**Sources:**
- (Results 2.1) "In order to understand how pre-vaccination transcriptional state is associated with post-vaccination antibody response, we performed a DE analysis of high- vs. low responders in the pre-vaccination state, and identified 12 upregulated and 7 down-regulated RNAs in high responders; upregulated RNAs included THRB, BLZF1, MPIG6B, and CCR1 (Table S7)."
