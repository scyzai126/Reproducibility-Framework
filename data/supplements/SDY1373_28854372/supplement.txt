[S1] Workflow to assess vaccine-induced innate immune signatures. [S2] Samples were available from participants in the open-label, dose-escalation single-center phase 1 trial conducted at the UKE in Hamburg and included 20 healthy male and female participants between 18 and 55 years of age, which were enrolled into two dose groups, consisting of 10 vaccinees each. [S3] Group 1 received 3x106 pfu of the rVSV-ZEBOV vaccine, and group 2 received the highest dose of 20x106 pfu as a single injection on day 0. [S4] Blood samples from the vaccinees were obtained on day 0 (pre-vaccination), as well as days 1, 3, 7 and 14 after vaccination to analyze early vaccine-induced innate immune responses. [S5] Study participants were enrolled between November and December 2014 and followed up for 6 months. [S6] The study at the UKE was one of four harmonized investigator-initiated phase 1 trials coordinated through the VEBCON to assess the safety and immunogenicity of ascending doses of rVSV- ZEBOV in Gabon, Kenya, Germany and Switzerland, and to facilitate rapid progression to phase 2 and 3 trials in affected countries. [S31] SSC-A FSA-A SSC-A FSC-A SSC-W SSC-A FSC-A FSC-W FSC-A LIVE/DEAD DCs/Monocytes NK cells Singlets FSC-A CD3/CD19/CD14 CD56 CD16 NK cells CD56 bright CD56 dim HLA-DR CD3/19/56 CD16 CD14 CD11c CD303 Monocytes and DCs mDCs and pDCs A B C Supplemental Statistical Information Partial Least Squares (PLS) regression gathers a dimension-reduction approach together with a regression method. [S32] It is suited for the analysis of datasets with a large number of variables and a relatively small sample size and searches the optimal linear combinations of multidimensional, highly correlated, explanatory variables (here innate immune response variables), which best explain the variability in the multivariate outcome (here antibody responses). [S33] The linear combinations of variables are summarized in latent components. [S34] The sparse version, sPLS, allows for variable down-selection by including a Lasso penalization (Le Cao et al., 2008, Boulesteix and Strimmer, 2007, Liquet et al., 2012, Liquet et al., 2016). [S35] The number of PLS components and the number of variables per component were optimized using the PLS Q criterion and the cross-validation prediction error (mean squared error of prediction (MSEP), leave-one-out), respectively. [S36] This method allowed for down-selection of early innate markers explaining most of the variability in the antibody responses (Liquet et al., 2012, Liquet et al., 2016). [S37] The predictive value of the multivariable linear model was assessed by the root square residuals (RSR), which is the ratio of the root mean square error between predicted and observed values to the standard deviation of the observations. [S38] Missing values of cell surface markers were imputed by non-parametric simple imputation based on Random Forests, a method that takes into account the multidimensional and correlated nature of the immune markers (Breiman, 2001).Values of cytokine concentrations below the standard curve were also imputed, based on Gaussian Kernel estimation of the density function windowed between 0 and the corresponding Low Level Of Quantification (LLOQ) of the standard curve. [S39] The cytokine marker selected by sPLS (IP-10 plasma concentration on day 3) was not concerned by this imputation. [S40] For the analyses of differential gene expression after vaccination, we used the voom/limma pipeline, an empirical Bayes estimation of a linear model accounting for the inherent heteroscedasticity of RNA-seq data pipeline for differential expression analyses of RNA Seq data (Law et al., 2014). [S41] We first applied a filter to exclude genes with very low mean count from statistical comparisons (exclusion of genes with mean raw count <5) and then used TMM (Trimmed Mean of M-values) normalization and logCPM (logarithm of counts per million reads) transformation. [S42] TMM allows for between-sample normalization under the null hypothesis and serves as correction factor for library size in the analyses (Robinson and Oshlack, 2010). [S43] The voom method relies on linear modelling of logCPM transformed normalized data, with TMM library size correction, with an estimation of mean–variance trend through a LOWESS curve. [S44] This non-parametric approach avoids the negative binomial distribution assumption by instead estimating precision weights. [S45] Using these weights, the limma empirical Bayes analysis pipeline, based on the normal linear model, can then be applied to RNA Seq data. [S46] We tested the null hypothesis of log2 fold change =0 (compared to Day 0) for each gene, and used the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure for controlling FDR to account for multiple testing (Benjamini Y, 1995). [S47] Multiplicity-adjusted p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.
